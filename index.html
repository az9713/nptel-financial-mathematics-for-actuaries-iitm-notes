Learning route and prerequisites

The central task is to compare payments made on different dates. Begin by fixing the unit of money, the valuation date and the rate convention. The same discipline then carries from a single deposit to an annuity, a loan, a bond and a portfolio.

The assumed mathematics is algebra, exponentials and logarithms, geometric series, and basic differentiation and integration. Finance terminology and actuarial symbols are introduced in the chapters. Chapter 17 introduces the probability concepts needed for random returns, including variance, covariance and correlation.

Read in chapter order for the full route. For a specific calculation, identify its cash-flow pattern first, then use the corresponding chapter. Check the valuation date and rate conversion before substituting numbers. An annuity-immediate and an annuity-due have different first payment dates; a quoted nominal annual rate still needs its conversion frequency; a yield to maturity and a spot rate answer different questions.

Each chapter's source note distinguishes lecture material, research extensions and original teaching constructions. Timestamp links locate the relevant recording. Numerical examples state their rounding rules; the Python listings reproduce selected calculations and expose their assumptions. Compare a computed answer with its equation of value and limiting cases before treating it as meaningful.

The exercises combine derivations, computations, assumption checks, counterexamples and decisions. Attempt the argument before opening its solution. The solution's reasoning matters as much as its final amount or rate: it shows which assumptions make the answer valid.
